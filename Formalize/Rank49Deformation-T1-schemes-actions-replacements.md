# Rank-49 deformation T1 — exact schemes, symmetries, and replacements

**Status.** Proof task. This card specifies reusable Lean infrastructure; it does
not ask Lean to replay the rank-49 numerical certificate.

**Goal.** Give matrix-multiplication decompositions an exact public semantics and
prove that the standard symmetries, ordinary flips, reductions, and certified
local replacements preserve that semantics. The endpoint should support later
certificate replay without depending on random-walk code.

**Why now.** The rank-49 experiment uses several equivalence notions that must
not be conflated: equality of represented tensors, term permutation, termwise
gauge, matrix-multiplication sandwich isotropy, and the six cyclic/transpose
orientations. The existing Lean development has most of the tensor and flip
algebra, but not one stable package joining these notions.

## Statements to establish

### 1. Brent convention theorem

For rectangular dimensions, use the existing underlying representation

`Scheme k (n1*n2) (n2*n3) (n3*n1) r`.

Define explicit row-major reshape/flatten equivalences from `Fin (m*n)` to
matrix entries through `finProdFinEquiv`, and define `E(a,b)`, matrix entry
notation, and factor shapes through those equivalences. An ordered scheme then
represents

`sum_l U_l tensor V_l tensor W_l`.

Prove directly against `BilinearComplexity.matMulTensor` that equality with

`sum_(a,b,c) E(a,b) tensor E(b,c) tensor E(c,a)`

is equivalent to every coordinate Brent equation

`sum_l U_l[a,b] V_l[b',c] W_l[c',a']
   = delta(b,b') delta(c,c') delta(a,a')`.

The order `W[c',a']` is binding. Separate two scalar-extension statements:

- base change of `sumTensor`, Brent equality, and the resulting `RankLE`, valid
  for the appropriate unital ring map;
- preservation of the stronger `Scheme.Valid` predicate, whose nonzero and
  distinct-summand fields require an injective map, in particular a field
  embedding.

A valid length-`r` scheme gives `RankLE r`; it does not prove minimal rank.

### 2. Exact action semantics

Define the following actions on ordered schemes and prove that each preserves
the represented matrix-multiplication tensor when its hypotheses hold:

- permutation of the `r` terms;
- termwise unit gauges `(lambda_l, mu_l, nu_l)` with
  `lambda_l * mu_l * nu_l = 1`;
- sandwich transformations
  `(U,V,W) -> (P U Q^-1, Q V R^-1, R W P^-1)`;
- cyclic rotation `(U,V,W) -> (V,W,U)` with the corresponding dimensions;
- transpose/reversal `(U,V,W) -> (V^T,U^T,W^T)`;
- the six orientations generated by cyclic rotation and reversal.

Package a witness so that replay checks the invertible matrices, orientation,
term permutation, gauges, and all mapped factor equations. Do not identify the
tensor isotropy group with the stabilizer of a particular decomposition.

### 3. Local replacement theorem

Define a replacement certificate by a finite set `I` of removed slots, a
finite ordered list `L` of inserted rank-one terms, and an exact equality
between the removed and inserted local tensors. Set

`r' = r - |I| + length(L)`.

Make the output `Fin r' -> TriadData` deterministic: retain unaffected old
terms in increasing original-slot order, followed by inserted terms in list
order. Equivalently, expose certified embeddings of surviving old slots and
inserted-list positions into `Fin r'`. Prove:

- replacing certified local terms preserves `sumTensor` and hence the represented
  tensor equality;
- preservation of the stronger `Scheme.Valid` additionally follows when the
  inserted evaluated terms are nonzero and remain globally distinct from one
  another and from unaffected terms;
- rank bookkeeping follows `r' = r - |I| + length(L)`;
- replacements compose when every later removed-slot set is transported through
  the preceding replacement's surviving-slot and insertion maps;
- a tensor symmetry transports a replacement certificate;
- replay of the witness is sufficient; no search provenance is needed.

Term order remains operational data even though the represented tensor uses a
sum. Canonicalization is a separate operation.

### 4. Flips and reductions as instances

Recover all three elementary flip identities from the replacement theorem,
including inverse flips and the projective refactorization layer already used
by `Phase1Schemes.lean` and `Phase2Algebra.lean`.

Prove the field reduction criterion: if a set of terms spans a one-dimensional
space in one factor position and is linearly dependent in another position,
then the same local tensor has a decomposition with one fewer term.

For `F_2`, prove the completeness statement used by the ordinary flip index:
every ordinary nontrivial two-term flip has a repeated nonzero projective factor
in one of the three positions, and every algebraically legal such flip is found
by repeated-factor enumeration. Since `F_2` has one nonzero scalar,
projective equality of nonzero factors is literal equality.

This completeness statement is only about ordinary two-term flips. It must not
be stated as a classification of arbitrary finite replacements.

## Proof route

1. Keep the generic tensor equality independent of matrix multiplication.
2. Express sandwiching as three compatible invertible leg maps and discharge
   the matrix-multiplication identity by index calculation.
3. Build cyclic and reversal actions from explicit reindexing maps, then prove
   the six-orientation closure.
4. Make the replacement theorem a finite-sum decomposition lemma.
5. Instantiate the existing flip identities as small replacement witnesses.
6. Reuse the shared-factor linear-dependence arguments for reduction.
7. Isolate the two-term `F_2` classification as its own theorem with explicit
   nonzero and nondegeneracy hypotheses.

## Acceptance criteria

- The public theorem statements do not depend on `Scratch` namespaces, even if
  their proofs initially reuse scratch lemmas.
- A small explicit scheme can be transformed and its witness replayed with
  `native_decide` or ordinary simplification.
- The action witness distinguishes equality, equivalence, and stabilizer
  membership.
- The ordinary-flip completeness theorem has a visibly narrow two-term scope.
- No theorem calls a length-49 presentation a proof of tensor rank 49.

## Non-goals and hazards

- Do not encode the 49-term table in this task.
- Do not formalize a random walk, RNG, cache ordering, or pool policy.
- Do not use a hash match as equality evidence.
- Do not treat term permutation or outer orientation as infinitesimal tangent
  directions.
- Do not introduce a generic hot-loop coefficient-domain interface; this card
  concerns mathematical semantics, not runtime dispatch.
